## What the anatomy showed

Activation-library read, deflate decode and validation remains dominant at roughly 1.79 seconds in the final sample.
The next material costs are primary/fallback decay parsing (roughly 0.69 seconds combined), input hashing (0.22
seconds), and material network preparation (0.22 seconds). The full in-memory 951,393,048-byte cross-section array explains most of the
roughly 1.08 GB peak RSS. Persisted prepared data, target-selective loading and mapping remain P15 work by protocol.

The retained P14 changes remove only demonstrated redundant work:

- ordinary runs no longer allocate uncertainty derivatives they never consume;
- the flux denominator is collapsed once, and leading/trailing zero-flux groups are skipped without changing the
  order of nonzero floating-point operations;
- ENDF exponent and fixed-width tail parsing avoid known failed/general parse paths;
- cross sections are checked while decoded rather than by a second full-memory scan;
- an NPZ that has already matched a declared SHA-256 avoids redundant per-member CRC work, while inputs without a
  declared digest retain the ordinary CRC-validating reader; and
- opt-in stage timing remains absent from ordinary result JSON and standard output.

Both the production example and a compact generated fixture match the opening binary recursively after removing only
top-level `ms`. Certificate-hash, inventory-value and ledger-value plants each fail the comparison. Regression tests
also require bit-identical reaction rates and ledgers between derivative and derivative-free assembly and require
invalid decoded cross sections to fail closed. No `unsafe`, physics, evaluated-data, public-schema or cache-format
change is included.
