The impurities that matter

Real steel carries traces of elements nobody asked for. Most don't matter. A few decide whether a part can be handled, or thrown away, decades later. This chapter checks them all at once and turns the answer into a purchase limit.

Steel comes with a certificate listing what's in it. Beside the main ingredients it lists impurities, each with a range: "cobalt 0.02 to 0.2%", say. The maker guarantees the range, not the exact amount.

In chapter 2 you saw cobalt-59 turn into cobalt-60. That was one impurity, checked by hand. This steel's certificate lists seven:

What are these three quantities?

Dose rate at the surface is the dose rate right up against a thick piece of the steel. ACTINV uses it to compare materials quickly; it's not the dose for a real shape. Clearance index compares every nuclide with the level below which a regulator lets the material go as ordinary scrap: 1 means just at the line, above 1 means not yet. Activity counts decays per second in each gram.

Step 4 of 7

Why the sliders are instant

The sliders didn't run ACTINV each time you moved them. They didn't need to. At a fixed neutron field, what each element turns into doesn't depend on what else is in the steel, so the answer for any mixture is the matrix's answer plus each impurity's effect times how much of it there is.

So actinv impurities runs the matrix once and each element once, sixteen runs in all, and then knows every steel the certificate allows. Here is the dose rate at 10 years for the typical amounts:

Add it up, then check it against a full run.
Why can an impurity's effect be negative?

Adding 0.1% of an impurity takes 0.1% of iron out, since iron makes up the rest. If the impurity makes less radioactivity than the iron it replaces, the total goes down a little. Nitrogen and boron do that for the dose rate.

          What ACTINV does differently
          
Every trace element, every time, in one command. actinv impurities takes the certificate's ranges and gives each impurity's share at each time, the worst steel the certificate allows, and the most of each impurity you can accept for a limit you set. It works out each answer from a run per element and then checks every reported number with a full run of its own, so you get both the speed and the proof.
