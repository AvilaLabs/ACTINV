Planning maintenance

After shutdown, crews go in to cut, unbolt and inspect. The longer they wait, the lower their dose, but every day of waiting is a day the machine isn't running. This chapter finds the balance.

Two parts sat two years in chapter 1's neutron field: the steel bracket, with 0.1% cobalt, and a 5 kg copper cooling pipe. Three jobs are planned, done one after the other:

Two rules protect the crew. Nobody enters while the dose rate where they'd work is above the access limit, 3 mSv/h here. And nobody gets more than 20 mSv from the whole outage.

The dose rates are the ones from chapter 1, 1 m from each part. All numbers come from actinv maintenance on those runs. The command is new: it comes with the next ACTINV release.

Why does the pipe hardly change?

Copper's activation is dominated by cobalt-60, made by fast-neutron reactions on copper, and cobalt-60 halves only every 5.3 years. Its dose rate is low from the start, so the pipe can be cut on day one. The bracket starts higher, and its short-lived part decays over the first months.

Step 4 of 7

What waiting costs

Rules say when the crew may go in. When they should is a trade. Waiting longer lowers the crew's dose, a little, while the machine sits idle. Many organisations put a money value on dose for exactly this: what they'd spend to avoid one person-sievert. ACTINV adds up both costs for every wait and picks the cheapest allowed one.

Why is the cheapest wait usually the earliest one?

Here the dose falls slowly: waiting a month more saves the crew a few person-millisieverts, worth little next to 60,000 of outage. Only when dose is valued very highly does a longer wait pay. With other parts, faster-decaying nuclides or a cheaper outage, the answer changes. That's why it's worth computing.

          What ACTINV does differently
          
From dose rates to a plan, in the same tool. actinv maintenance reads the results you already have, one per place, and answers when each job may start, what each worker and crew receives at every wait, the earliest wait that meets every limit, and the cheapest one when you put a price on dose and downtime. The plan is a short JSON file, so changing a job or a limit and planning again takes seconds.
